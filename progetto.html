The project’s primary objective is to develop a detailed conceptual model of the aquifer recharge processes in the Lombardy plain. In this region, characterizing the recharge areas primarily means characterizing the upper plain, where all recharge sources for the groundwater system originate—including those affecting the middle and lower plains. For this reason, the project focuses primarily on the upper plain. More specifically, the objectives are to identify the main recharge processes in the various plain hydrostructures (ISS, ISI, ISP) (with greater emphasis on the ISS), dividing the hydrostructures into subareas based on the identified recharge processes, and to quantify the percentage contribution of each recharge source and the recharge flows (m³/year) within the identified subareas. Another aspect to be examined in detail is the relationship between recharge processes and nitrate concentration in the aquifer, especially in areas with higher nitrate concentrations (hot spots). The quantitative assessment of recharge is based on experimental data collected (and currently being collected) as part of the project through: dedicated sampling of wells and surface water bodies, and repeated flow measurements over time in rivers and streams. These data will be supplemented by the results of previous studies conducted by the project proposers. The approach will be based on the acquisition of historical piezometric time series from regional authorities and, if possible, from water service providers. The project calls for a diversified application of these approaches within the study area.
